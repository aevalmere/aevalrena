**2026-09-14 Wave 3: Smash-faithful input**

- 2026-09-14 input: a chord binding is two or more codes joined by `&` (e.g. `KeyF&KeyJ`). Reason: pad axis codes already use `+` (`A0+`, `A1-`), so `&` was free to mean a chord without clashing with either device's own code format.
- 2026-09-14 input: F is the smash modifier and G is the tilt modifier, each shipping two-key defaults on P1 (F&J/F&W/F&S for fsmash/usmash/dsmash, G&J/G&W/G&S for ftilt/utilt/dtilt). Reason: gives every smash and every tilt its own direct key on keyboard on top of the existing direction-plus-Attack table, without displacing it.
- 2026-09-14 input: P1's W is bound to both Up and Jump, and holding Walk suppresses the jump a Jump-that-is-also-Up key would otherwise fire on a fresh Up press. Reason: without that suppression, holding Up always tap-jumps and up tilt (and forward tilt, which needs Walk too) would be unreachable from a keyboard where Up doubles as Jump.
- 2026-09-14 sim: Shield in the air air dodges toward the held direction; Shield on the ground never rolls or spot dodges. Owner rule. Reason: keeps Shield a single-purpose button, leaving rolling and spot dodging to the Dodge button and the double-tap.
- 2026-09-14 sim: out of shield only Jump, Grab, Attack (grab, or up smash when Up is held or the tap reads as a vertical smash), and Special + Up (up special) come out; every other attack, special, C-stick direction or direct-move key is dropped. Reason: matches Smash's out-of-shield options and keeps a held shield from being a free way to throw out any move.
- 2026-09-14 sim: a shortcut (its own key or a chord) that lands within `SHORTCUT_REPLACE_FRAMES` (3) frames of a button-started attack replaces that attack. Reason: the two keys of a chord are never physically simultaneous, so without a short replace window the first key down would already lock in the wrong move before the second key completed the chord.
- 2026-09-14 sim: only the up smash and up special shortcuts (plus Up + Attack, Up + Special, or C-stick up) pressed during jumpsquat cancel the jump into their grounded move; every other shortcut stays buffered and comes out in the air once the jump starts. Reason: matches Smash's jump-cancelled smash and up-special tech without a separate input path for it.
- 2026-09-14 tuning: roll retuned to 24 total frames, dodging (`highDodge`) on frames 3 to 16, 60 px of travel. Reason: a shorter commitment with the same share of the move spent dodging, and more distance to make a roll worth using for repositioning now that it only blocks non-low hits rather than granting full invulnerability.
- 2026-09-14 ai/ui: CPU level 0 is a training dummy that never presses Attack, Special, Grab, Shield, Dodge, Taunt, the C-stick, or Down, and stands still unless the match rule `cpuZeroMoves` (the Mode screen's "Lv 0 CPU" row) is on, in which case it wanders: walking, running and jumping at random, turning back from ledges and recovering toward the stage when knocked off. Reason: a match-wide rule instead of a per-slot Wander option keeps Character Select's CPU control down to one level slider.

Reason: v1 and v2 saves predate chords, Walk and the W/F/G layout, and merging them field by field would leave players with a mix of the old and new bindings instead of the clean new layout.
- 2026-09-14 sim: Walk gates smashes off. A direction flick within the smash tap window still resolves to a tilt, not a smash, while Walk is held, because a walking stick is tilted, not flicked; only a flick without Walk, the C-stick, or the F chords reach a smash.

**2026-09-28 Aevalmere CPU**

- 2026-09-28 ai: CPU level 10, "Aevalmere", is a separate brain in `src/ai/aevalmere.ts`, not a tenth row of the profile table. `cpuInput` sends every level of 10 or more to `aevalmereInput`, and the legacy `profileFor` still clamps to 1..9, so levels 0 to 9 are unchanged. Reason: the owner wanted a genuinely intelligent CPU rather than a stat-maxed level 9, and a deterministic sim at about 1.5 us per step makes FightingICE-style rollout search affordable.
- 2026-09-28 ai: Aevalmere decides by forward simulation. It copies the state into pooled scratch states (`copyGameStateInto`, which learns the field lists from the live object and never touches the real `stats`) and plays each candidate plan with `stepGame` for 16 to 60 frames against 2 to 5 opponent reply models chosen from the opponent's situation and weighted by its habits. It scores damage, KOs, projected KOs against the live blast rect, recovery feasibility and frame advantage, then picks by a blend of worst case and expected case. Reason: the rollouts are exact to the sim, so punishes, combo follow-ups and recoveries are checked rather than guessed.
- 2026-09-28 ai: the reaction floor is 4 frames. Aevalmere plans on the state from 4 frames ago, played forward with the opponent holding what it held, so it cannot see anything the opponent started in the last 4 frames. It also never presses a fresh grounded shield while an opponent's move is 0 or 1 frames old. Reason: the owner's humanizing rule (no frame-0 reactions, no visible input reading), and the FightingICE practice of planning on a delayed state rolled forward.
- 2026-09-28 ai: Aevalmere sends moves as direct codes (the same shortcut and command keys a player can bind), never taunts, and holds back any direction press that the sim's own tap timer would read as a double-tap roll. Reason: exact, unambiguous inputs with no smash-versus-tilt misreads, played within the 2026-09-14 input rules.
- 2026-09-28 ai: no-whiff rule. A committing plan is only taken if a rollout shows it connecting in some reply, or shows it safe when the opponent shields (for projectiles: safe against the rushing reply). An edgeguard is only taken if its rollout gets back to the stage, and a Final Smash only once it catches in every reply. Reason: the owner's "never whiff on purpose" and "edgeguard offstage only when it gets back".
- 2026-09-28 ai: a true-combo table is precomputed from Aeval's move data at module init: starter x percent bracket (0-30/30-60/60-100/100+) x victim spot, using the sim's knockback and hitstun formulas, with chains of up to 4 hits ranked by damage and by whether they end past the blast rect. It is rebuilt when the knockback tuning or the blast rect changes. The table only orders the candidates; the rollouts confirm each hit against the victim's real trajectory. Reason: SmashBot-style `framesleft >= framesneeded` punish knowledge, without trusting a table over the sim.
- 2026-09-28 ai: humanizing.
